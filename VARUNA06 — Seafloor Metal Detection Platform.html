01 / DESCENT

Deep-ocean exploration is expensive before it's ever conclusive.

Every square kilometre of seafloor costs time, fuel and specialised equipment to survey — and a single sensor pass rarely tells you enough to justify what comes next. VARUNA06 asks a narrower question: can a low-cost platform help identify the zones worth a closer look, before the expensive exploration begins?

↓ CONTINUE THE DESCENT
02 / WHY WE STARTED

Can a low-cost platform tell us where to look, before the expensive search begins?

Deep-sea resource exploration — for polymetallic nodules, hydrothermal sulphides, cobalt-rich crusts and rare-earth-bearing sediments — is costly, data-intensive and technically demanding. Depth, pressure, motion and background noise all work against a single confident reading, and every promising signal still needs to be checked again before anyone trusts it.

That's the gap VARUNA06 is built for: not to replace deep-water research platforms, but to give a fast, low-cost first read on where a closer, more expensive investigation is actually worth sending.

03 / THE PROBLEM

PS26064: four reasons preliminary seafloor investigation stays slow and expensive.

COST

Deep-ocean exploration needs complex infrastructure and vessel time — every hour on site is expensive before a single anomaly is confirmed.

SIGNAL NOISE

Underwater measurements are affected by motion and environmental conditions, so a raw reading rarely speaks for itself.

FALSE POSITIVES

A single sensor response, on its own, usually isn't enough to justify committing to a detailed follow-up survey.

VERIFICATION

Promising anomalies need repeated measurements from different positions before anyone can act on them with confidence.

↓ NEXT: SENSING
10 / MULTI-PHYSICS SENSING

One platform, several ways of reading the seafloor.

Electromagnetic TX/RX coils sense conductive metallic anomalies beneath the seabed surface.

The magnetometer measures local magnetic field disturbances associated with ferrous or magnetic material.

Galvanic/electrical sensing reads conductivity and resistivity contrasts in seabed sediment.

The acoustic sensor provides seafloor structure and backscatter information.

The onboard camera gives visual confirmation once the platform is close to a target.

Depth, pressure, temperature and IMU data provide the environmental context used to correct every other reading.
